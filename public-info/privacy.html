Privacy notice

Updated September 27, 2026. This notice describes a personal, single-user integration operated by its account owner for their own Oura data.

Data and purpose

The owner may upload Apple Health workout summaries through Health Auto Export. The app retains workout identifiers (hashed), type, timing, duration, distance and active calories; supported workout heart-rate, speed, power and cadence samples are retained historically. GPS routes and Apple Health step samples are discarded. Compact performance statistics and recent workouts and selected comparable historical episodes within 180 days, with available surrounding recovery measurements, may be sent to OpenAI; full workout sample arrays are not sent to OpenAI. These workouts are included in scheduled notifications and optional OpenAI context. Re-uploads update matching identifiers; Apple Health deletions do not automatically remove imported records.

With the owner's authorization, the app reads available daily sleep, readiness, activity, stress, sleep measurements, SpO2, and workout data. It uses these measurements to create summaries and detect changes. It also stores the Oura account identifier to validate incoming events and OAuth tokens to maintain authorized access. Optional permissions can be declined.

Storage and processing

At the owner’s request, workout labels are retained and included in optional OpenAI analysis so summaries can refer to workout names.

The app runs on the owner's DigitalOcean server. OAuth tokens are encrypted in its database; the encryption key is held separately in server configuration. Retained summary inputs are not encrypted at the application level. The app retains supported workout heart-rate samples but does not retain GPS routes or full raw export payloads. Health data is not displayed on these public information pages.

Notifications and providers

Summaries are sent to the owner's configured ntfy server and topic. The ntfy provider and phone notification infrastructure may process and retain message content. Notifications are not end-to-end encrypted and may appear on the phone lock screen. Private-notification mode can replace metrics with a generic update notice. Topic access restrictions must be configured separately.

Oura provides the source data, DigitalOcean hosts the app, and the configured ntfy service delivers notifications. The app does not sell data or use it for advertising. When the owner enables optional OpenAI analysis, selected measurements, personal baseline statistics, recent workout context, and any manually copied cycle information are sent to OpenAI to generate recovery, workout, and sleep observations. Raw traces, locations, account identifiers, and credentials are excluded. The integration requests store:false, but OpenAI's applicable API retention and abuse-monitoring policies still apply; this is not a guarantee of zero retention. The latest analysis is cached on the server and may be included in private ntfy notifications. The owner can disable future AI calls in server configuration.

Retention and control

Summary inputs and the most recent AI analysis remain in the app database until the owner deletes them. Optional cycle observations can be removed through the private status page; previously delivered notifications and provider records are not deleted by that action. The owner can revoke authorization in Oura, stop the service, and delete its database volume and any backups. Deleting local data does not delete messages previously delivered to ntfy; those follow the notification provider's retention settings.

Website and contact

These public pages use no analytics or tracking cookies. Hosting and network providers necessarily handle requests and connection information. The private account-connection flow uses a temporary security cookie. Since this integration serves only its operator, privacy requests and configuration changes are handled directly by that owner through their server and Oura account. No third-party users are accepted.
